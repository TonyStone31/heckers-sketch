Radiant heat layout

PEX loops laid out over a slab, a manifold to a zone, with the material list, a heat load if you want one, and a submittal to hand in and to lay the tube from.

Open it from Shop > Radiant heat layout... or type /radiant (/pex and /hydronic do the same). Select the floor first. Nothing goes on the drawing until you press Build it, and Cancel or Esc leaves the drawing as it was.

What it does

You give it the floor and where each manifold hangs. It lays the tube in rows at the spacing you ask for, parallel to the manifold's wall, in loops that go out and come back on neighboring rows. Every loop is a round trip from the manifold and back, and its length - both leads included - is kept under the longest loop you allow. No loop crosses another, and the tube stays 6" off every wall and obstacle.

It does not lay one layout and stop. It searches: it tries many layouts for each zone and keeps the best ones, ranked by how much of the floor they reach, how close the loop lengths come to each other, and how easy they are to lay. You pick the one to build, or take the best.

Before you start

  1. Draw the floor. Any closed shape works - a rectangle, an L, a room with a curved wall.
  2. Split it into zones by drawing lines across it. Every face is a zone, with a manifold of its own. A barn drawn as one big rectangle with lines across it is four faces, so four zones.
  3. Cut out what the tube must stay out of - a column, a shaft, a chase - so it is a hole in the floor's face. The tube is routed around it.
  4. Select the floor. Clicking the faces, a drag, or Ctrl+A all work: only faces are used, and the lines that come along are ignored.
  5. Open the wizard.

A face that is the same shape as a hole in another selected face is left out on its own - it is the column drawn inside the slab, not a zone. With nothing selected, the wizard does not open and says to select the floor first.

The settings are remembered from one job to the next; the job name is remembered for the floor it was given to. Changing the tube, the spacing, the longest loop or the most loops a manifold throws away every zone's layout, since the tube can no longer go where it went: search again.

The slab is not asked about. The wizard takes a 4" concrete slab, the tube centered in the pour, R-15 under it, and says so on the material list and the submittal.

Zones and manifolds

The plan in the middle shows the floor north up, with a compass in the corner. Each zone is outlined, holes are shaded gray, and each manifold is a small numbered box - the one you are working on is yellow. Once a zone is searched, every loop is drawn in its own color, so you can follow one run by eye.

When the wizard opens, it puts a manifold in every zone, on a wall and a foot in from it, toward the middle of all the zones - where the boiler most likely is. Suggest manifolds puts them all back there.

Tabs

Under the plan there is a tab for each zone, and All zones when there is more than one. Click a zone on the plan and its tab comes up. A zone's tab has:

  • Name - Kitchen, barn east bay. It shows on the tab, the plan, the material list, the submittal and the zone's group on the drawing, and is remembered for that floor.
  • Keep the manifold where I put it - see below.
  • A line saying where the manifold is and how the zone stands: not searched yet, or the size of manifold its layout takes, how many layouts were searched in how long, and how installer friendly it is. Before you search, it warns you when your coverage goal is out of reach for this tube, or when the zone wants more loops than one manifold takes.
  • Search this zone, Clear it (throws away its layout), and Not a zone.
  • Layouts found, best first - see picking a layout.
  • Material list - this zone's. On the All zones tab, every zone's together, with the totals.

Moving and turning a manifold

  • Drag a manifold's box to move it. It stays inside its own zone.
  • Drag it within about a foot and a half of a straight wall and it turns to lie along that wall - and keeps that heading when you drag it away again. That is the quick way to turn one and then move it out into the floor.
  • Right-click it for Rotate manifold 90 degrees, or Square the manifold: long side east - west, north - south, at 45 degrees, or at 135 degrees.

Moving or turning a manifold throws away that zone's layout. The other zones keep theirs.

Keep the manifold where I put it

When a search is stuck, as a last resort it may slide the manifold along its wall a few feet, and the material list says how far it moved it. Tick Keep the manifold where I put it when the spot you picked is the only place it can hang. It is remembered for that zone.

Not a zone, and bringing it back

A closed shape in the selection that is not floor to heat - a chase, a column against a wall - can be left out. Pick its tab and press Not a zone, or right-click it on the plan for Zone N is not a zone - leave it bare. It turns gray on the plan, gets no tube, and stays out the next time you open this floor. The last zone cannot be left out.

To bring them back, right-click the plan: Bring back the shapes left out. Every one left out comes back as a zone, with its manifold where Suggest would put it.

The plan's right-click menu

Right-click a manifold, or inside a zone, and the menu acts on that zone:

  • Search zone N, and Search all zones.
  • Zone N is not a zone - leave it bare, and Bring back the shapes left out.
  • Rotate manifold 90 degrees, and Square the manifold.
  • Clear zone N, and Clear all zones - their layouts, not the zones.

Searching

A search takes seconds a zone on a real floor, longer on a big one, so nothing is searched until you ask. Press Search this zone on its tab, Search all zones, or use the plan's right-click menu. A zone not searched shows no tube, and Build it stays off until every zone has been searched.

What a search does

  1. It lays a set of layouts first: the rows both ways, the first loops given more or less of the longest loop, different distances for the tube to fan out from the manifold. With no goals, that is the whole search.
  2. With goals, it keeps going: more layouts tried at random - loops of different lengths, lanes nudged, rows turned - until the best one meets the goals, or you stop it, or it reaches its give-up time.
  3. Along the way, and more as it gets stuck, it may even up the rows at the far wall by closing them up an inch or so, lay loops with hooks at the far wall, and - unless you told it not to - slide the manifold along its wall. The material list and the submittal say when any of this was done.

A layout only meets the goals when it covers enough, its loops are within the spread you gave, no join runs through an obstacle, it needs no more loops than a manifold takes, and it is friendly enough to lay (below). Each search starts from a new random point, so searching a zone again can find a different layout.

When a coverage goal is more than the tube can reach - say twelve loops of 300 ft at 9" on a 3,000 sq ft zone - the zone's tab says so before you search, with what to change: a wider spacing, a longer loop, more loops a manifold, or the zone split. The search then works toward what the tube can reach.

The search window

While it searches, a window stays up over the wizard. It shows which zone it is on (and which of how many), a bar, the layouts laid a second, and the best so far - how much it covers, how close its loops come, and how many loops. Everything in it can be changed while it runs:

ControlWhat it does

Give up afterNever, or 1, 5, 15 or 30 minutes, or 1 hour, a zone. Past it, the zone keeps the best it found and the next zone starts.

Found so far, best firstThe layouts it has kept. Click one to see it drawn beside the list and to keep it as the zone's layout; double-click one to stop and keep it.
Stop - keep the bestEnds this zone with the best it has, and goes on to the next zone.

Give up after, less friendly and hooks are remembered for next time. While the window is up, the wizard cannot be changed or closed.

Picking a layout

A search keeps up to 24 layouts a zone. Each line in the list, best first, reads:

  • how much of the floor it covers, percent;
  • how far apart its loops are - how much shorter the shortest is than the longest, percent and then feet;
  • how many loops, and how many bends;
  • how installer friendly it is, of 100;
  • ok when it meets the goals.

Click a line in the zone's Layouts found list and that is the layout the zone shows and will build. The material list says which of how many it is.

Installer friendly

Each loop is scored out of 100 for the person laying it. A plain serpentine block, set out from two measurements, is 100. A loop loses points for every measurement past two it takes to set out - where its rows stop short of a wall, and where its lead runs - for bends past a plain serpentine's, and for reaching out past its own block of floor into the others'. A layout's score is the average of its loops'.

The gauges and the material list

Two bars over the plan, over the zones searched so far:

  • Coverage - how much of the floor the tube actually reaches.
  • Evenness - how close the loop lengths come, in the zone where they are furthest apart, as a percent and in feet.

Green is good, amber is part way, red is poor.

The material list for a zone gives the tube and spacing, the floor area, the loops, the manifold's size and about how much wall it takes, every loop's length, and then:

  • total tube with no waste, and the order with the waste added;
  • ties or staples, one every 15";
  • how many bends, and how much of the tube runs in long straights;
  • how far out from the manifold every tube is on the grid;
  • whether the turn at the end of each row - the spacing - is tighter than the tube bends, and which PEX (A, or B and C) can make it;
  • any join that runs through an obstacle - route those by hand;
  • floor not reached, the slab assumed, how friendly it is to lay, and the search's record;
  • SHORT OF THE GOALS, OUT OF REACH or MORE LOOPS THAN ... A MANIFOLD IS SET TO TAKE where that is so, with what to change.

The line in red just above Build it says what stops a build: a size that does not read, or a zone with no layout.

The heat load

Heat load... opens a window of its own. It is optional: a layout builds without it, and the submittal only carries a heat load once you use one. Once you do, the button reads Heat load - in.

It is a simplified heat loss - walls, glass, doors, ceiling, the slab's edge and the air that leaks in - at the code U-values for the climate zone, worked out as you type. The answers are remembered for that floor.

What it works out

For the zone you are on:

  • the loss by where it goes - walls, windows, doors, ceiling, slab edge, floor down, air - and the total in BTU/hr;
  • what the floor has to give, BTU/hr a square foot, and its surface temperature. Past what a floor gives at 85 F it says SHORT, and how much has to come from other heat;
  • the water: supply and return temperatures, and the zone's GPM;
  • the loop that needs the most head, its GPM and feet of head - once the zone has been searched;
  • the copper pipe size from the header to the boiler;
  • the pump: GPM at feet of head.

And the boiler for all the zones: the heating, 5% for the piping, the hot water if it makes it, the altitude derate, the rated output it needs at least, the input at about 95% efficiency, how far down it should modulate (the smallest zone), and the snow melt, alone and with one boiler doing both.

Use it puts the heat load in the submittal. Leave the heat load out takes it out. Cancel keeps what was there before.

The submittal

Export the submittal... saves the job as a PDF - on letter paper, or A4 for a metric drawing - or as plain text if you pick a .txt file. It is named for the job to start with. Preview the PDF writes the same PDF where nothing is kept and opens it in your PDF reader, so you can look it over first.

It covers the zones searched so far; a zone not searched says it has no layout. In order:

  • The system - the tube and spacing, the longest loop allowed, the zones and manifolds, the loops, the floor and how much of it the tube reaches, the tube and the order, ties, the goals, how many layouts were tried in how long, installer friendly, and the slab.
  • Material list - the tube to order, the manifolds by size with the wall each takes, ties or staples, and the insulation under the slab.
  • Zones - a line each: floor, loops, manifold, wall space, tube, reached, loops within, bends, easy, and whether it met the goals.
  • The plan of every zone, north up, with a scale bar.
  • A page for each zone - its plan with the manifold dimensioned to the walls, a table of its loops (length, measurements to set out, color), what it takes, the heat, water and pump when there is a heat load, anything out of the ordinary (the manifold moved, rows closed up, a tight turn, short of the goals, too many loops), and how it was found.
  • A setting-out sheet for every loop, a loop to a page for the binder: the loop by itself in its color with the others faint, the dimensions to mark it out from the walls - to the first row, across the rows, to where the rows turn, to the far wall, each lead from its nearest wall - where the manifold goes, a warning when it takes more than two measurements, a scale bar, and an installer checklist: rows marked, tube laid and tied, both ends tagged, supply and return connected, pressure tested, flow set (to the design GPM when there is a heat load), then laid by, date and checked by. Every loop of a zone is at the same scale.
  • Heat load, water and the boiler, when one is used - the design conditions, the boiler, a line for each zone, where the loss goes, what was asked, anything to watch (a floor short of its load, water over 120 F for a slab, a pump past 25 ft of head, snow melt without glycol), how it was worked out, and every table value used, with its source.
  • Design record - for each zone, when it was searched, how many layouts in how long, what happened on the way (stopped by hand, gave up, picked by hand, goals met at which layout), and the layouts it kept, the chosen one marked.
  • How this layout was made - the rules the layout keeps, in a few lines.

Every sheet has a title block: the job, the date, the program's version, and sheet N of M. A scale bar reads true even printed "fit to page", where a stated scale would not.

Building it

Build it puts the layout on the drawing exactly where the floor is - nothing to place afterward - and leaves what it built selected. It builds the layouts the wizard showed; it does not search again. For each zone:

  • A group named for the job (or Radiant), the zone and its name, and what was laid - the tube, the spacing, the number of loops and the total length: 1/2" PEX at 9" o.c. - 11 loops and so on.
  • Inside it, every loop a group of its own, named Z1 L3 with its length. Click one and you have the whole run from port to port; the entity panel gives its length. Each loop is drawn in its own color, as on the wizard's plan.
  • The manifold as a box, turned the way it hangs, in a group of its own - Z1 manifold - 11 loops.
  • A labels group: each loop's name and length, no tube - obstacle on every hole, and a note by the manifold with the zone's summary. /hide labels and /show labels put them away and back.

The runs are reference lines, like a dimension's own line: they draw in full but never close faces with the floor. Undo takes the whole build back in one step.

After it is built

The drawing keeps the whole job in the zones' groups - the settings, every zone and manifold, the names, the heat load and each zone's layout. Click a zone's group and right-click it for:

  • Open in the Radiant Wizard... - the wizard, titled as it was built, on the job just as it was: its zones, manifolds, settings, names and layouts, searched already. Change what you like. Build it puts the new layout in place of every one of the job's old zone groups, in one undo step; Cancel leaves the drawing alone. A zone whose group was erased comes back not searched.
  • Print the Submittal Again... - the submittal as it was built, PDF or text, with no search and no wizard.

Only the layout each zone built keeps its loop paths. The other layouts its search kept are kept as a record - how much they covered, their bends and lengths - for the design record. Pick one of those in a reopened wizard and it says Only the built layout was kept - search again for the rest.

Layouts built before 28 September 2026 did not keep their job, so these two items do not show for them. Select the floor and run the wizard again.

Limits

  • It is not an engineered design. The layout is geometry at the spacing and lengths you gave it. The heat load is a simplified estimate for choosing the boiler, the pumps and the pipe - not a Manual J, and not a room-by-room load calculation where a permit asks for one. Check the design against the tube and manifold maker's instructions and the code where the job is.
  • The search is bounded. It does not promise to reach every corner of an odd floor; the material list says how much was not reached.
  • The slab is not asked about: 4" thick, tube centered, R-15 under it.
  • One manifold to a zone. A zone that wants more loops than a manifold takes is for you to split.
